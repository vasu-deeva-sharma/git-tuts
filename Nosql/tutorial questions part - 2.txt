UNIT–III: Document Databases
1. Explain how transactions work in document databases and describe the limitations of transactions involving multiple operations.
2. Explain the query features of document databases, including MongoDB queries and CouchDB views.
3. Explain the concepts of read scaling and write scaling in document databases, including the role of sharding.
4. Explain the major use cases of document databases and identify situations in which document databases may not be suitable.
5. Explain how replica sets improve the availability of MongoDB databases.
UNIT–IV: Column-Family Data Stores
1. A college application stores student profiles and examination orders. Apply the column-family concept to organize these two types of related data.
2. Explain the concept of a Column-Family Data Store with a suitable example.
3. Design a customer data structure containing different columns for two customers with non-uniform information using a Column-Family Data Store.
4. Explain the difference between a Standard Column Family and a Super Column Family.
5. Design a Cassandra data structure for storing student academic details, including student ID, name, department, semester, and examination marks. Illustrate the structure with a suitable example.
6. Describe the structure of a column, row, and column family in Cassandra.
7. Apply the concept of Super Columns to organize a customer's address and billing information in Cassandra.
8. Describe the concept of Replication Factor and explain its importance in Cassandra.
9. Apply the concept of Replication Factor to determine how data is distributed when the replication factor is set to 3 in a Cassandra cluster.
10. Explain replication factor and illustrate its role in storing data across multiple nodes.

5. Explain the role of N, R, and W parameters in Riak.

7. What is consistency in a graph database?
8. Construct a graph for five employees working on two projects. Show how to identify employees who worked on the same project.
